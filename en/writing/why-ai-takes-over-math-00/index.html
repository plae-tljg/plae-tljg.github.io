    00 Collection Abstract: Why I Already Knew AI Would Take Over Mathematics

    2026-09-24·2 min readDraft preview

    Why this collection exists: a judgement made in January 2024, and why it did not come from AI capability.

    AI数学问题意识合集

    This article was translated from the Chinese original by an AI. Wording and terminology may be off.

    In January 2024, GPT-4 was not yet smart. It got plenty of undergraduate mathematics problems wrong, let alone research. I was already certain that AI would take over mathematics.

    That judgement did not come from AI capability. If it depended on “AI is already very smart,” I should have waited until it was smarter before drawing a conclusion. My certainty came from another direction — from mathematics itself: from how mathematics is learned, how it is done, and which part of it is a composition of operations and which part is choice.

    These four articles are a retracing. They do not state the answer on the first page; instead they walk the judgement again in the order I originally arrived at it.

    The first, “Three Principles of Mathematical Learning,” is the foundation of the whole manuscript. It was originally a standalone guide to learning mathematics, written in May 2026, kept here as it stands with only typographic tidying. It covers three things: generation from one, mechanical deduction, concept collision; how a free structure is pressed into a new object by constraint; and the move called “vertical leap.”

    The second, “Preparation: Several Ways of Looking at Mathematics,” lays out the viewpoints used later: the staircase, mechanical deduction, proof-as-search, the flesh-and-blood method, the equality of operations, physicists versus academy, and the vitality of AI talking nonsense. They are a vocabulary, not conclusions.

    The third, “Why AI Will Take Over Mathematics,” starts from the judgement of January 2024 and runs all the way to 2026. What it has to do is this: under these viewpoints, see clearly which part of mathematics a machine can take over and which part it cannot.

    The fourth, “Problem-Consciousness: The Layer AI Cannot Take Over,” asks about the other half: if part of it can be taken over, what is left, and who holds it.

    This is not a book about how powerful AI is, nor a book about how beautiful mathematics is. It wants to answer a narrower question: why, in January 2024, with a GPT that was not smart, was I already certain that AI would take over mathematics.
