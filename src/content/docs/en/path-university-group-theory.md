<p class="intro">Group theory studies the pure form of closed operation systems. It emerges from a shift in perspective: instead of calculating on objects, we now study “the way of calculating” as a new object itself.</p>

<h2>The Four Axioms</h2>
<div class="concepts-grid">
<div class="concept-card card">
<h3>Closure</h3>
<p>Operation result never leaves the set</p>
</div>
<div class="concept-card card">
<h3>Associativity</h3>
<p>$(ab)c = a(bc)$</p>
</div>
<div class="concept-card card">
<h3>Identity</h3>
<p>$e \cdot a = a$</p>
</div>
<div class="concept-card card">
<h3>Inverse</h3>
<p>$a^{-1} \cdot a = e$</p>
</div>
</div>

<h2>Important Theorems</h2>
<div class="theorem-card card">
<h3>Lagrange’s Theorem</h3>
<p>The order of a subgroup divides the order of the group.</p>
</div>
<div class="theorem-card card">
<h3>Sylow Theorems</h3>
<p>Precise characterization of subgroups of prime power order.</p>
</div>
<div class="theorem-card card">
<h3>Galois Correspondence</h3>
<p>Field extensions correspond precisely to subgroups of the Galois group.</p>
</div>
