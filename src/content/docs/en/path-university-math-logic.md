<p class="intro">Mathematical logic corresponds to the Second Principle: mechanical deduction. When proving, you can temporarily ignore content and just follow the form.</p>

<h2>The Core of Logic: Form Itself</h2>
<div class="concept-card card">
<p>The core of logic is form itself. Given a string of symbols, it transforms symbols according to rules without caring what the symbols represent. For example, Modus Ponens: from “if $P$ then $Q$” and “$P$”, directly infer “$Q$”.</p>
</div>

<h2>Spatial vs Temporal Nature</h2>
<div class="two-columns">
<div class="concept-card card">
<h3>Spatial</h3>
<p>Commutative law: $P \land Q \equiv Q \land P$ - two propositions side by side, order doesn’t matter.</p>
</div>
<div class="concept-card card">
<h3>Temporal</h3>
<p>Transitivity of implication: $P \to Q, Q \to R \Rightarrow P \to R$ - clearly a chain of steps, a path from start to finish.</p>
</div>
</div>
